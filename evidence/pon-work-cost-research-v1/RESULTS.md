owner-supplied external pins. Known public producer optimizations materially

work cost, a probabilistic upper tail, or production security of pure matrix PoN.

included in the honest baseline. Keep the hybrid construction as an explicitly
conditional security comparison. No difficulty, reward, consensus or readiness

## Counting and cost definitions

An opportunity is identified semantically; a different proof envelope alone does
not create another paid lottery opportunity. Relation validity, parent eligibility,
native admission and canonical chainwork are separate predicates. Comparisons
reuse task/challenge streams across arms and are counterfactual observations.
Counts below are sums of within-cohort distinct observations unless stated otherwise;
they are neither globally unique mining trials nor additive serial chainwork.

The reported opportunity-rate ratio is `R_opp = (M_A / C_A) / (M_H / C_H)`.
Here `M` includes completed distinct opportunities and `C` includes actual measured
cost, including the stopping overshoot. This ratio is not an estimate with a proved
error bound for `E[N_A(B)] / E[N_H(B)]`. Budget-winning rates additionally require
the winning candidate to have completed production by `B`.
Inclusive production completion is subsequently confirmed by the observer; it is
not a claim that native admission finished before `B`. Core stage charges and
inclusive producer cost are reported separately. Failed work is not refunded.

The general integer arm is not uniformly faster. In the full-rank cold comparison,
generic seed 202/sample 0 spent 18,574,669 ns in two preparation calls, completed
one proof and incurred 18,958,049 ns core cost against a 16-ms budget. Its matching
integer cohort completed 24 proofs. The inclusive paired ratio is 23.26381x.
This observation is retained; its cause is unresolved. The pooled 1.29749x must not
be described as a stable 29.75% speedup. Pooled ratios below two also do not imply
that every pair stayed below two or four.

`cold` means actual reconstruction, not CPU/OS cache flushing. `batch` is sequential,
not SIMD or GPU execution. The general integer arm reuses an existing exact paired
multiplication identity; it does not invent a new multiplication algorithm.
Its online logical product counts are 168,960 versus 327,680 for prepared generic
and 458,752 for scalar original. Existing field-paired work already has the same
product-count reduction. Different operand widths, additions and reductions make
these counts unsuitable as hardware speed claims or computational lower bounds.

32 attempts, representing 16 global semantic opportunities, with zero winners.
Its success probability is zero, not `2^-256`; per-winner cost is undefined.

## Kappa targets and the conditional hybrid comparison

Research targets `11/10`, `5/4`, `2` and `4` were selected before measurement.
They are sensitivity thresholds, not existing consensus security parameters.
The cold structured observations do not support blanket 1.1x/1.25x engineering
parity against ordinary generic. No universal bound at any of the four levels is
established. A finite ratio above a threshold is not itself a formal rejection of
a bound on expected budget-winning tickets. Public improvements must also update
the honest best-known baseline, rather than being assigned only to an adversary.

The [exact conditional calculations](../../formal/pon-nakamoto-v1/experiments/w1_cost_security_math.py) give
`kappa_combined <= 1 / (beta/kappa_H + (1-beta)/kappa_U)`.
This requires comparable complete opportunities, additive component costs, binding,
all failed work charged, and no omitted shared preprocessing or overlapping work.
`beta` is the reference cost fraction of the independent floor, not a target weight.
The inequality alone is not an output-count or upper-tail theorem.

| Desired kappa, assuming kappa_H = 1 | Minimum beta if kappa_U = 4 | Minimum beta if useful acceleration is unbounded |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 11/10 | 29/33 | 10/11 |
| 5/4 | 11/15 | 4/5 |
| 2 | 1/3 | 1/2 |
| 4 | 0 | 1/4 |

The experiment has not proved `kappa_U <= 4` or a physical `kappa_H = 1`.
With unknown useful cost, the conditional limiting advantage is `kappa_H/beta`.
Neither random-oracle query bounds nor a correct proof certify joules, hardware
parity or non-amortization across all tasks. Average rates do not supply chain
security without conditional tail control and an honest-growth argument.
